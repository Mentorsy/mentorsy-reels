Scratch is not a toy that gets outgrown. It is the same ideas without the punctuation.

Loops, conditionals, variables, events, sequence: those are the concepts a first-year computer science course teaches, and a nine year old can hold all of them while dragging blocks. What text adds is syntax, and syntax is not the difficult part.

Parents often push a child out of blocks early, worried it looks unserious. The usual result is a child fighting missing semicolons instead of thinking about structure, and concluding they are bad at coding.

The right moment to move across is when the blocks become the slow part, not when the calendar says so.

A child who can debug a Scratch project can debug anything. The rest is typing.
